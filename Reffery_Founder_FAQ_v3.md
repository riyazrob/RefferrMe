# Reffery — Founder Questions & Answers (v3)

---

### 1. What is Reffery?

Reffery is an AI-powered professional referral marketplace that connects job seekers with employees who are genuinely willing to provide internal referrals.

Unlike traditional job boards or networking platforms, Reffery makes the employee referral process structured, transparent, and efficient through AI-assisted candidate evaluation, trusted professional profiles, and end-to-end referral tracking.

---

### 2. What problem does Reffery solve?

Today's hiring process has three major problems:

- Candidates struggle to find employees willing to give genuine referrals.
- Employees receive hundreds of random LinkedIn messages from unqualified candidates.
- ATS systems rely heavily on keyword matching instead of understanding real experience.

Reffery solves all three by intelligently matching candidates to referrers while reducing the workload on employees.

---

### 3. Why is this problem important?

Employee referrals consistently produce higher-quality hires and often lead to faster hiring than other channels. But finding a genuine referral remains fragmented, manual, and dependent on personal networks. Reffery standardizes and simplifies this.

---

### 4. Why is now the right time to build Reffery?

Several trends converge to make this the right moment:

- AI can now understand resumes and job descriptions semantically instead of relying only on keyword matching — this wasn't practically possible even three years ago.
- Companies continue to prioritize employee referrals because they consistently produce higher-quality, faster hires.
- The job market is increasingly competitive, making a genuine referral more valuable than ever to a candidate.
- Professionals already use LinkedIn for networking, but no platform is purpose-built around structured, trusted employee referrals specifically — the behavior exists, the dedicated tool doesn't.

Put simply: there's a gap here that, as far as we've found, no one has filled yet — a focused platform for genuine referrals, not another networking or job-search app competing for attention.

---

### 5. Why are you the right founder to build this?

I came to the U.S. as an international student on a student visa, and my first job came through a referral from my professor — not through a job portal application. That experience made the gap personal and concrete: job postings are visible, but hard to land through, because ATS scrutiny is intense and a hiring manager can easily miss things in a resume that weren't highlighted clearly enough. A referral bypasses that entirely — it gets a real person to actually look.

I didn't arrive at this idea abstractly. I lived the exact failure mode Reffery is designed to fix, and the product decisions since then — the cold-start plan, the MVP scope, the validation gates — come from that same firsthand understanding of where hiring actually breaks down, not just where it looks broken from the outside.

---

### 6. Who is your target audience?

We are starting with **one wedge, not five**: backend and cloud software engineers (2–8 years of experience) at 15–20 target companies where we can seed initial referrer supply from founder networks.

We deliberately are *not* launching broad across SWE, Data, Cloud, DevOps, and PM simultaneously — a two-sided marketplace needs supply and demand to meet in a narrow lane first, or referrers get flooded with mismatched requests and churn. We expand to adjacent roles only once density (see Q12) is proven in this first cohort.

---

### 7. How big is the opportunity?

We are intentionally starting with one niche: software engineers in the IT industry — narrow on purpose, per Q6.

Once the model is validated there, it naturally expands, because employee referrals are used across nearly every industry, not just tech. Beyond individual candidates and referrers, our customer groups include hiring managers, talent acquisition teams, recruiters, and enterprise HR departments — each of whom benefits from better-qualified referral pipelines even though our initial wedge focuses on the candidate/referrer marketplace first.

Our long-term vision is to become the infrastructure for employee referrals and referral intelligence across industries — not just a tool for software engineering job seekers. The initial wedge is intentionally small; the addressable model is not.

---

### 8. How does Reffery work?

**Referrer**
- Sign in with LinkedIn
- Verify work email
- Post referral opportunity, upload Job Description
- AI extracts requirements
- AI ranks candidates
- Review applications, refer selected candidates
- Track hiring progress

**Candidate**
- Sign in with LinkedIn
- Upload resume, build profile
- Request referral
- AI evaluates profile
- Referrer reviews request
- Referral submitted, progress tracked

---

### 9. What makes Reffery different from LinkedIn?

LinkedIn is a networking platform; Reffery is a dedicated referral marketplace. On LinkedIn, candidates find employees manually, send cold messages, and hope for a reply. On Reffery, employees voluntarily accept referral requests, and AI filters and ranks candidates before they ever reach the employee.

LinkedIn optimizes for networking and engagement. Reffery optimizes for successful employee referrals. Those are different products with different success metrics — which is also why LinkedIn building a competing feature wouldn't automatically replicate what Reffery does (see Q11).

We also don't treat LinkedIn as core infrastructure we're fully dependent on — it's used for professional identity, authentication, and profile discovery. Reffery is designed to complement LinkedIn rather than compete with it: LinkedIn provides trusted professional identity, while Reffery manages the referral workflow itself — AI-assisted evaluation, endorsements, referral tracking, and reputation, all of which live natively in Reffery. If the opportunity arose, we'd welcome a strategic partnership with LinkedIn to make posting and requesting referrals even more accessible — but the platform's core value doesn't disappear if that partnership never happens or API access changes.

---

### 10. What makes Reffery different from an ATS?

ATS platforms manage recruiting workflows. Reffery focuses on referral intelligence: understanding resumes semantically, extracting mandatory requirements from job descriptions, evaluating the full candidate profile, and explaining *why* a candidate matches — rather than keyword filtering. The referral decision always belongs to the employee.

---

### 11. Why should companies and employees trust AI recommendations?

Reffery's AI never makes hiring decisions. It helps employees prioritize candidates by providing explainable recommendations — showing *why* a candidate is ranked where they are, against the specific requirements extracted from the job description. Every referral decision remains entirely under the employee's control; the AI narrows the pile, it doesn't decide who gets referred. This directly addresses the most common concern about AI in hiring — that it replaces human judgment rather than supporting it.

---

### 12. Why wouldn't ATS companies copy this?

Our initial advantage is execution and focus. AI resume-to-JD matching alone is not defensible on its own — an ATS incumbent could plausibly ship a version of it in a quarter.

Our long-term moat will be built through a trusted referral network, proprietary referral outcome data, and professional reputation scores that compound over time — assets that are easy to copy on day one and hard to copy once they have real density. Concretely: the AI improves after every completed referral by learning from successful referrals, interview outcomes, and hiring trends, so match quality compounds with real usage data rather than staying static like a model an incumbent could clone off the shelf. Our job in the first 6–12 months is to reach that density before a well-resourced competitor decides the space is worth entering.

---

### 13. Why wouldn't LinkedIn build this?

LinkedIn optimizes for networking and engagement; Reffery optimizes for successful employee referrals. Those are different products with different success metrics, and Reffery is purpose-built around a narrower job — opt-in referrals with AI ranking and full outcome tracking. This is a plausible feature bet for LinkedIn, not an impossible one; our advantage is focus and speed, not an unbuildable technical moat.

---

### 14. How does the AI work?

The AI reads resumes and job descriptions, identifies mandatory and preferred requirements, evaluates technical skills, experience, certifications, and soft skills, and produces an explainable, ranked fit assessment. It recommends — it never decides.

---

### 15. How will Reffery solve the cold-start problem?

We launch with a waitlist before the app goes live. The landing page lets professionals register as a **Referrer**, a candidate wanting to **Receive Referrals**, or **Both**.

Initial supply and demand come from: founder personal networks, Instagram/LinkedIn audience, university alumni, and employees at the 15–20 target companies from Q6.

**Launch gate (not just "enough"):** we don't open the app until we hit roughly **50+ verified referrers across 15+ companies** and at least a **3:1 candidate-to-referrer ratio** on the waitlist within the initial wedge. Below that ratio, referrers get flooded with requests they can't act on and churn immediately — so we treat this as a hard gate, not a soft goal. These are current best estimates and will be revised once we have real waitlist data.

---

### 16. Why would employees use Reffery?

They get AI-ranked candidates instead of hundreds of unsorted messages: less spam, faster review, better candidate quality, a professional reputation boost, a way to help others, and easy tracking of who they've referred.

---

### 17. How do you prevent spam?

**At MVP:** daily referral limits, LinkedIn authentication, work email verification.

**Post-MVP, as we scale:** AI spam detection, reputation scoring, community reporting. These are roadmap items that become necessary once volume creates the spam problem in the first place — not claimed as live on day one.

---

### 18. How do endorsements work?

Candidates request endorsements from former managers, team leads, directors, colleagues, professors, or clients. Endorsers sign in with LinkedIn, verify work email, confirm the professional relationship, and provide a structured recommendation. Endorsements are labeled as such — not treated as guaranteed proof of employment.

---

### 19. How are fake profiles prevented?

**At MVP:** LinkedIn login, work email verification, basic resume validation.

**Post-MVP:** community reporting, AI fraud detection, full reputation system.

---

### 20. How does referral tracking work?

Both candidate and referrer can update status through: Requested → Accepted → Submitted → Application Received → Recruiter Reviewing → Interview Scheduled → Interview Completed → Offer Received → Offer Accepted → Joined, with Rejected and Withdrawn as terminal exit states reachable at any point in the flow.

---

### 21. Why don't you want users on the app all day?

Reffery isn't a social platform — the goal is successful referrals, not engagement. Once a user secures a job, they update their status so they're no longer surfaced in active recommendations.

---

### 22. What happens after someone gets hired?

They mark themselves as employed and are removed from active recommendations. Later, they can become a Referrer and help others — a self-sustaining loop we intend to validate with real cohort data on hired-to-referrer conversion, not assume outright.

---

### 23. What is your business model?

Freemium.

**Free:** limited referral requests, basic search, AI summary.

**Premium:** daily or weekly passes — higher referral request limits, AI profile analysis, detailed AI job matching, advanced filters. Pricing is intended to stay affordable for people actively job-searching, not a recurring subscription burden.

---

### 24. Why daily pricing instead of monthly?

Many job seekers are unemployed or actively transitioning, and a monthly subscription creates pressure unrelated to their actual usage pattern. Daily/weekly passes let people pay only during active search windows. This is a hypothesis, not yet validated — see Q29.

---

### 25. What are the biggest risks?

- **Cold-start marketplace** — supply and demand need to meet in a narrow wedge before expansion. *Mitigation:* hard launch gate (Q15), narrow initial audience (Q6).
- **LinkedIn API limitations** — sign-in and data access depend on a platform we don't control. *Mitigation:* design core flows (profile, resume upload) to not be fully dependent on LinkedIn data if access changes.
- **Building trust** — both sides need confidence the other is genuine before participating. *Mitigation:* work email verification, reputation system, phased rollout of trust features (Q17/Q19).
- **Enterprise adoption** — the long-term enterprise tier depends on companies trusting an outside referral layer. *Mitigation:* prove the consumer marketplace first; enterprise is explicitly a "future" phase, not a launch dependency.
- **AI accuracy** — bad matches erode trust with referrers fast, since they're the ones spending review time. *Mitigation:* AI is explicitly advisory, not a filter that hides candidates; referrers see the full pool and the reasoning, not a black-box shortlist.

### 26. What are your Year 1 success metrics?

*(Directional targets — to be refined once we have real waitlist and usage data.)*

- Verified referrers onboarded
- Active candidates on the platform
- Referral requests submitted
- Referrals actually submitted by referrers (the real conversion point)
- Hires resulting from Reffery referrals (the North Star, per Q31)
- Paid users on daily/weekly passes

These aren't final numbers — they're meant to show we think in terms of execution and funnel health, not just vision.

**By quarter:**

- **Months 1–3:** Launch landing page, build waitlist, run pricing validation (Q29), recruit first referrers in the target wedge (Q6).
- **Months 4–6:** Launch MVP, onboard first cohort, hit the launch gate from Q15, collect initial referral data, iterate on AI ranking quality.
- **Months 7–9:** Expand to additional target companies, introduce premium tier, tighten referral tracking, start measuring referral-to-hire conversion as the real signal of product-market fit.
- **Months 10–12:** Look for early product-market fit indicators, open enterprise conversations, begin ATS integration pilots, and evaluate expansion beyond the initial software-engineering wedge.

We're not committing to hard dates for enterprise or expansion — those are gated on hitting real usage and conversion numbers in the earlier quarters, not calendar time.

### 32. What is your long-term vision?

We're not just building a referral app — we see Reffery as three connected products that build on each other:

1. **Reffery Marketplace (B2C)** — candidates, referrers, endorsements, referral tracking. This is where we start, because it's the fastest way to validate the core problem.
2. **AI Hiring Intelligence (B2B)** — semantic resume evaluation, job requirement extraction, candidate ranking, recruiter dashboards. This grows naturally out of the AI we build for the marketplace.
3. **Referral Infrastructure (Platform)** — APIs, enterprise integrations, referral analytics, hiring insights, for companies who want referral intelligence built into their own hiring stack.

We start focused, prove the marketplace works, and let the same AI and referral data become the foundation for the enterprise layer later — rather than trying to build all three at once.

The throughline across all of it: **we measure success by successful careers, not screen time.** Most platforms optimize for more sessions and more engagement. We optimize for better referrals, faster hiring, and users leaving because they succeeded.

### 33. What is Reffery's mission?

To help professionals earn genuine employee referrals through AI-assisted matching, trusted professional relationships, and transparent referral tracking.

---

### 34. Why should investors believe this will work?

Reffery addresses a proven, high-quality hiring channel — employee referrals — with a focused solution, rather than competing with LinkedIn as a social network or replacing ATS platforms outright. If the platform consistently increases successful referrals that convert into hires, it creates value for users immediately and can expand into enterprise hiring intelligence later. The open risks are cold-start density (Q15) and payment validation (Q29) — we see these as the two things to prove first, not assume.

---

### 35. Aren't there already referral apps in the market — how are you different?

A few exist, but none do what Reffery does. Most existing referral apps function more like LinkedIn replacements — another feed, another networking layer — rather than a focused tool for one job: getting the right candidate to the right referrer. They typically lack structured endorsements, and the process of actually finding and requesting a referral isn't simple.

Reffery is deliberately narrow. We don't want to be a place people spend time — we want them to get a referral, land the job, and stop needing the app. That's a different design philosophy from competitors optimizing for engagement, and it shapes product decisions throughout (see Q21 on why we don't want daily engagement).
